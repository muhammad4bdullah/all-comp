CHAPTER 7 — ENTREPRENEURSHIP
7.1 Entrepreneurship
Concept of Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship is the process of creating and developing an enterprise or business.

It involves:

Identifying opportunities
Taking calculated risks
Making investments under uncertainty
Innovative planning
Making business decisions
Organizing resources to achieve business objectives

Entrepreneurship requires a combination of skills and qualities such as imagination, innovation, risk-taking ability, planning, and resource management.

Entrepreneurship as a Career

A person can turn their hobby or passion into a profession. This can provide both personal satisfaction and financial benefits.

“Do what you like, or like what you do.”

An entrepreneur should also act as an ethical member of the community and consider their responsibilities toward society.

7.2 Entrepreneur

An entrepreneur is a person who starts, owns, organizes, and manages a business while taking the associated financial and business risks in the hope of earning profit.

Entrepreneurs also move economic resources from areas of lower productivity to areas of higher productivity and greater yield.

Characteristics of Entrepreneurs
1. Desire for Responsibility

Entrepreneurs have a strong sense of personal responsibility for the outcomes of their ventures.

They prefer to control and properly utilize their resources to achieve self-determined goals.

2. Preference for Moderate Risk

Entrepreneurs are calculated risk-takers, not reckless gamblers.

They analyze possible risks and rewards before making decisions.

3. Self-Reliance

Entrepreneurs often have to perform multiple roles, especially during the early stages of a startup when there are few employees.

4. Confidence in Ability to Succeed

Entrepreneurs have confidence in their ability to achieve their goals.

This confidence is based on choosing suitable strategies and working toward success rather than simply being overconfident.

5. Determination / Grit

Grit is the ability to remain focused on achieving a long-term goal despite difficulties and unfavorable circumstances.

Successful entrepreneurs demonstrate strong determination when facing challenges.

6. Desire for Immediate Feedback

Entrepreneurs want to know how well their business is performing.

They use feedback to:

Identify weaknesses
Measure progress
Correct problems
Improve their business
7. High Level of Energy

Starting and running a business requires long hours, hard work, and commitment. Therefore, entrepreneurs need a high level of energy.

8. Future Orientation

Entrepreneurs focus on the future and actively search for new opportunities.

They plan ahead and organize resources to build their businesses.

9. Value of Achievement Over Money

Money is not necessarily the main motivation of entrepreneurs.

Entrepreneurs may be primarily motivated by achievement, success, innovation, and accomplishing their goals. Money can serve as a measure or symbol of achievement.

Benefits of Entrepreneurship
Provides self-fulfillment
Provides potential for profit
Allows people to work in areas they enjoy
Contributes to society and the economy
Creates new products, services, and opportunities
Can make a positive difference in the world
Drawbacks of Entrepreneurship
Risk of losing financial investment
Long working hours
Hard work and high workload
Lower quality of life during the early stages
High stress levels
Complete responsibility for important decisions
Business may take time to become established
7.3 Design Thinking

Design thinking is an iterative, non-linear process used to understand problems and develop effective solutions based on user needs.

It consists of five phases:

1. Empathize

Understand the users, their needs, experiences, and problems within the context of the design challenge.

2. Define

Clearly identify the problem and create an actionable problem statement.

3. Ideate

Generate as many possible ideas and solutions as possible.

4. Prototype

Build a simple model or sample of the solution to learn quickly, cheaply, and early.

5. Test

Collect feedback from users and use it to refine the solution and better meet user needs.

Design Thinking Sequence

E → D → I → P → T

Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test

Iterative and Non-Linear

Design thinking does not always follow the stages in a fixed order.

A design team can:

Repeat stages
Work on stages in parallel
Return to an earlier stage
Continuously improve the solution

The team uses results and feedback to review and improve its assumptions and solutions.

7.4 Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

A Minimum Viable Product (MVP) is a basic version of a product containing enough features to test an idea with users and obtain feedback.

It allows entrepreneurs to test an idea early without spending excessive resources.

7.5 Business Plan

A business plan is a written summary of an entrepreneur's proposed business venture.

It includes:

Operational details
Financial details
Marketing opportunities and strategies
Management skills and abilities
Business goals
Risks and strategies

A business plan provides a systematic and realistic evaluation of a venture's chances of success.

Importance of a Business Plan
Evaluates the Venture
Helps determine the chances of success in the market.
Identifies Risks
Helps identify the principal risks facing the business.
Provides a Game Plan
Acts as a plan for managing the business during startup.
Measures Performance
Allows actual results to be compared with targeted performance.
Attracts Capital
Helps attract investors and obtain funding.
Business Plan as a Roadmap

A business plan acts as a roadmap showing:

Where the business wants to go
How it intends to get there
What resources and strategies are needed
Business Plan Creation Tools
LivePlan

LivePlan is a business-planning tool that provides templates and asks users questions about their business. Based on the answers, it helps create a complete business plan.

PlanMaker

PlanMaker is a spreadsheet program that can be used for financial calculations and business planning.

PlanRight

PlanRight provides tools for creating business plans that can be presented to investors.

Canva

Canva is an online design tool used to create:

Graphics
Videos
Presentations
Social media content
WordPress

WordPress is a website creation and management tool.

7.6 Entrepreneurial Skills

Entrepreneurial skills are abilities that help a person:

Think effectively
Communicate
Interact with people
Solve problems
Make decisions
Manage resources
Identify opportunities

An entrepreneur should use these skills to identify problems and create useful products or services.

Ideas for a Small Business

An entrepreneur should consider the needs of their community and identify products or services that can satisfy those needs.

Questions to Consider
What needs exist in my community?
What problems are people experiencing?
What product or service could solve these problems?
Which of my skills can address these needs?
How can I use my skills to solve the problem?
Which needs could my colleagues and I develop into a business plan?
7.7 Understanding the Market

If a product or service is needed by people and they are willing to pay for it, there is demand.

Customers

People who need and are willing to pay for a product or service are customers.

Market

The number of potential customers for a product or service represents its market.

Competitors

Businesses that provide similar products or services are called competitors.

Market Survey

An entrepreneur can estimate the size and characteristics of a market by conducting a survey of customer needs, preferences, and willingness to pay.

Market Survey Questions
Who is interested in the product?
What do customers like or dislike about it?
Are customers willing to pay the required price?
Will they buy regularly or seasonally?
How many products/services will they need?
What should the product be like?
Where should it be available?
Who are the competitors?
7.8 Setting Up the Business Plan

Most businesses aim to make a profit from the products or services they sell.

Profit

Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price

Profit can be reinvested into the business.

Selling Price

The selling price should consider:

Cost of Production + Overheads + Profit = Selling Price

The final selling price should be realistic and reasonable.

Cost of Production and Overheads

The entrepreneur should accurately calculate all costs involved in producing and selling a product or service.

These may include:

Labour
Raw materials
Equipment
Depreciation of equipment
Other production costs
Overheads
Entrepreneur's salary
Labour Cost

Labour costs may depend on:

Worker knowledge and contribution
Experience
Qualifications
Basic wage rates
Government taxes
Social security contributions
Setting the Profit

The entrepreneur should decide a reasonable profit margin by considering the relationship between demand and supply.

Demand > Supply

If demand is greater than available supply, the price may be increased.

Supply > Demand

If supply is high but demand is low, the price may be reduced.

An excessively high profit margin can discourage customers.

When sales increase, an entrepreneur may reduce the profit margin and lower the selling price. This can help the business secure and expand its market.

7.9 Devising a Business Plan
Executive Summary

The executive summary is a concise overview of the main aspects of the entire business plan.

It is normally prepared after the complete business plan has been developed.

It should cover:

Business goals
Products/services
Costs
Timeline
Target audience
Major strategies
Remember:

Executive Summary = Short overview of the complete business plan.

Your Startup / Company

A business plan should explain:

Company
Team
Company hierarchy
Vision
Goals
Short-term objectives
Long-term objectives
Short-Term Goals

Goals intended to be achieved in the near future.

Long-Term Goals

Goals intended to be achieved over a longer period and provide direction for the future of the company.

SMART Goals

Business goals should be SMART.

S — Specific

The goal should be clear and precise.

M — Measurable

The goal should be quantifiable and assessable.

A — Achievable

The goal should be attainable.

R — Realistic

The goal should be practical and reasonable.

T — Time-Bound

The goal should have a specific time frame or deadline.

Memory Trick

SMART = Specific → Measurable → Achievable → Realistic → Time-Bound

7.10 Market Analysis

Market analysis is essential before starting a business.

An entrepreneur should study:

Current market conditions
Customer needs
Market trends
Demand
Supply
Competitors

Market trends can change, so entrepreneurs should continuously monitor the market and adjust their strategies when necessary.

SWOT Analysis

SWOT analysis is a technique used to evaluate a business, product, or idea in relation to its market.

S — Strengths

Internal advantages of the business.

W — Weaknesses

Internal disadvantages or limitations.

O — Opportunities

External possibilities that the business can take advantage of.

T — Threats

External factors that may negatively affect the business.

Memory Trick

SWOT = Strengths → Weaknesses → Opportunities → Threats

Products and Services

Products and services are a major part of a business plan.

The business plan should clearly describe:

What the product/service is
Its features
How it works
What it provides to customers
How it is different from alternatives

The entrepreneur should have a thorough understanding of their product or service.

Target Market

The target market is the specific group of customers a business intends to serve.

An entrepreneur should identify:

Potential buyers
Customer needs
Customer preferences
Competitors
Product differences
Potential threats
Competitive advantages

Entrepreneurs should develop strategies to attract and retain customers by providing quality products and services.

Marketing and Promotional Strategies

Businesses need effective marketing strategies to reach their target customers.

Digital Marketing Methods
Website Marketing

Promoting products and services through a website.

SEO — Search Engine Optimization

Improving a website's visibility in search engine results.

Video Marketing

Promoting products or services through videos.

Social Media Marketing

Using social media platforms to promote products and services.

PPC — Pay-Per-Click Marketing

Online advertising in which payment is generally based on clicks.

Content Marketing

Creating useful or relevant content to attract customers.

Email Marketing

Promoting products or services through email.

Affiliate Marketing

Promoting products through affiliates who may receive a commission for successful referrals.

7.11 Deciding the Budget

Every product or service requires time, money, and effort.

An entrepreneur should:

Estimate expenses
Measure resources being used
Consider business goals
Consider potential customers
Monitor financial performance

Budget requirements vary depending on the business and its target market.

Important Financial Statements
Balance Sheet

Shows the financial position of a business.

Cash Flow Statement

Shows money flowing into and out of the business.

Income Statement

Shows revenue, expenses, and profit or loss over a period of time.

Financial information should be reviewed periodically.

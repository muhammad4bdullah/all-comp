Chapter 6 — Safe and Responsible Use of Computer
6.1 Safe and Responsible Use of Computer

Safe and responsible computer use means protecting computer hardware, software, data, and users from damage, loss, unauthorized access, and security threats.

1. Keep Systems Updated

Keep the operating system, utilities, drivers, software, and firmware updated. Updates improve security and fix vulnerabilities.

2. Strong and Unique Passwords

Use passwords containing:

Uppercase letters
Lowercase letters
Numbers
Special characters

Use different passwords for different accounts and enable two-factor authentication (2FA) where possible.

3. Phishing

Phishing is an attempt to trick users into revealing personal or sensitive information through suspicious emails, messages, links, or websites.

4. Backup Data

Keep copies of important data on another storage device, external drive, USB, or secure cloud backup service.

5. Secure Wi-Fi

Avoid entering sensitive information, such as banking details, when connected to untrusted public Wi-Fi networks.

6. Careful Downloads

Download software and files only from trusted and reliable sources to reduce the risk of malware.

7. Privacy Settings

Use privacy settings to control what personal information is shared publicly.

8. Physical Security

Protect computers from theft, physical damage, dust, and harmful environmental conditions.

9. Safe Online Shopping and Banking

Use trusted websites and check for HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure) when entering sensitive information.

6.2 Responsible Use of Hardware and Software

Responsible use of technology involves efficiency, sustainability, security, ethics, and social responsibility.

Responsible Hardware Use
Extend Hardware Lifespan

Proper use and maintenance can extend the life of hardware and reduce electronic waste.

Maintain Hardware

Keep devices clean and protect them from physical damage.

Protect Data and Privacy

Use strong passwords, 2FA, and updated software and firmware.

Upgrade Responsibly

Do not replace or upgrade hardware unnecessarily. Upgrade only when it is required or provides a useful benefit.

Follow Manufacturer Guidelines

Follow instructions provided by manufacturers to prevent damage and ensure safe operation.

Responsible Software Use
Follow Laws

Respect copyright, intellectual property, and software licensing laws. Do not use pirated or cracked software.

Ethical Use

Do not use software for:

Hacking
Malware
Cyberbullying
Cyber harassment
Other harmful or illegal activities
Privacy and Data Protection

Be careful about what personal information is shared, where it is shared, and how it is used. Respect the privacy of others.

Intellectual Property

Respect copyrights, patents, and trademarks and give appropriate credit to creators and open-source projects.

Proper Licensing

Understand the requirements of different software licenses, including open-source and commercial licenses.

User Education

Digital literacy and education help users understand ethical, secure, sustainable, and responsible technology use.

6.3 Safe Use of Digital Platforms
Safe Use of Search Engines
Use trusted search engines such as Google and Bing.
Government websites and academic databases are generally reliable sources.

For sensitive information, use official and trusted websites.
Safe Use of Social Networking

Be careful about who you interact with.
Avoid sharing sensitive information publicly.
Think before posting or sharing content.
6.4 Laws to Protect User Privacy and Intellectual Property

Protecting user privacy and intellectual property (IP) is important because personal data is widely collected and processed, while creators need legal protection for their work and ideas.

Protection of User Privacy

Privacy laws give users greater control over:

Their personal information
How their data is collected and used
Who can access or share their information
Intellectual Property

Intellectual property refers to creations of the mind, such as inventions, artistic works, designs, and original ideas.

IP laws protect the rights of:

Authors
Artists
Inventors
Innovators

They help ensure creators receive credit and legal protection for their work.

Prevention of Unauthorized Use

These laws help prevent unauthorized access, copying, distribution, or misuse of personal data and intellectual property.

Important Computer-Related Laws

CFAA — Computer Fraud and Abuse Act
Prevents unauthorized access to computers and computer-related abuse.

GDPR — General Data Protection Regulation
Operates in the European Union and gives users greater control over their personal data and how it is used.

DMCA — Digital Millennium Copyright Act
Addresses copyright infringement involving digital content.

ECPA — Electronic Communications Privacy Act
Provides a legal framework concerning interception of electronic communications and access to stored electronic communications.

Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act
Operates in Australia and regulates interception of telecommunications and access to communications.

PIPEDA — Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act
Operates in Canada and regulates the collection, use, and disclosure of personal information by private-sector organizations.

Computer Crimes Acts
Various countries have laws addressing computer crimes such as unauthorized access, hacking, malware-related activities, and other computer-related crimes.

COPPA — Children's Online Privacy Protection Act
Operates in the United States and protects the online personal information of children under 13.

Cybersecurity Law
China has enacted a comprehensive cybersecurity law covering areas such as cyberspace activities, data protection, and critical information infrastructure.

6.6 Different Types of Malicious Software and Malware
Malicious Software (Malware)

Malicious software, or malware, is software intentionally designed to harm, exploit, damage, or gain unauthorized access to computer systems or data.

1. Computer Virus

A computer virus is a malicious program that can attach itself to files or programs and spread when the infected file is executed.

2. Ransomware

Ransomware is malware that prevents users from accessing their files or systems and demands a ransom/payment to restore access.

3. Trojan Horse

A Trojan horse is malicious software that disguises itself as legitimate or useful software to trick users into installing or running it.

4. Phishing

Phishing is an attempt to trick people into revealing sensitive information by pretending to be a trustworthy person or organization, often through fake emails, messages, or websites.

5. Hacking

Hacking refers to gaining unauthorized access to or manipulating computer systems, networks, or data.

6. Spam

Spam consists of unwanted, unsolicited, and often irrelevant messages, such as promotional emails or messages sent in large numbers.

7. Spyware

Spyware is software that secretly collects information about a user without their knowledge or consent.

8. Pharming

Pharming is redirecting users from a legitimate website to a fake or malicious website without their knowledge.

9. Cookies

Cookies are small text files stored on a user's device containing information about their interactions with a website.

Example: Cookies can remember login information and website preferences.

Cookies themselves are not malware; they are normally used for legitimate website functions.

10. Scam

A scam is a deceptive scheme designed to trick people into giving away money, personal information, or other valuable information.

11. Software Piracy

Software piracy is the unauthorized copying, distribution, or use of software without proper licensing or permission.

Types of Software Based on Availability
Freeware

Freeware is software available to use without paying a fee.

Freeware does not necessarily mean open-source because its source code may remain private.

Shareware

Shareware is software provided with a free trial or limited functionality. Users may need to purchase it to continue using it or access its full features.

Open-Source Software

Open-source software has source code that is publicly accessible and can generally be viewed, modified, and redistributed according to its license.

6.7 Information Privacy, System Security and Usability Trade-offs
Information Privacy

Information privacy means protecting personal information from unauthorized access, use, or disclosure.

It ensures that individuals have control over how their personal information is collected, stored, and used.

Security–Usability Trade-off

There is often a trade-off between system security and usability.

Higher security → more restrictions and complexity → lower ease of use.
Higher usability → simpler and easier access → potentially lower security.

Example: A system requiring strong passwords, 2FA, and frequent verification is more secure but less convenient.

Fake News and Disinformation
Misinformation / Fake Information

False or misleading information that is presented as factual, without necessarily intending to deceive or harm.

Disinformation

False or misleading information that is deliberately spread to deceive people.

Difference

Misinformation → False information, not necessarily intentional

Disinformation → False information + deliberate intention to deceive
